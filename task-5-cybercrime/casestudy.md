## Case Study: The Fake Bank Email Trap

### Background

Rohan, a 19-year-old first-year engineering student from Bhopal, received an email that appeared to come from his bank — SBI (State Bank of India). The email had the SBI logo, a professional format, and read: "Your account has been temporarily suspended due to suspicious activity. Click here to verify your identity and restore access immediately."

### How It Happened — Step by Step

1. **The bait:** Rohan received the email on a busy exam day when he was already stressed.
2. **The urgency:** The email warned that his account would be permanently closed in 24 hours if he did not act.
3. **The fake link:** He clicked the link, which opened a website that looked exactly like SBI's official site — same colors, same logo, same layout.
4. **The trap:** He entered his customer ID, password, and mobile OTP on the fake site.
5. **The damage:** Within minutes, Rs. 18,500 was transferred out of his account. The fraudulent site had captured all his credentials in real time and used them to log into his actual account.

### Who Is Targeted

Phishing attacks typically target students, elderly users, and anyone unfamiliar with how banks actually communicate. Banks never ask for passwords or OTPs via email or phone.

### Consequences

Rohan lost his savings, felt embarrassed, and spent weeks trying to file a complaint and recover the funds. The mental distress caused him to miss two internal exams. Most phishing victims never recover their money.
